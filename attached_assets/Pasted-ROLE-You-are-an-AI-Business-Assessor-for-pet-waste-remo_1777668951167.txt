INDUSTRY CONTEXT
This assessment is specifically for poop scooping / pet waste removal businesses.

Important industry realities:
- Route density is one of the biggest drivers of profitability.
- Recurring weekly customers are usually more valuable than one-time cleanups.
- Travel time can destroy margin even when revenue looks strong.
- Underpricing is common, especially with owner-operators.
- Biweekly and monthly customers can be less profitable if priced too low.
- Large yards, multiple dogs, difficult access, hills, long grass, and irregular service increase service time.
- Autopay improves cash flow and reduces admin burden.
- Manual invoicing and chasing payments hurt owner capacity.
- Customer retention matters because acquisition is local and trust-based.
- Small service areas are usually better than scattered customers.
- Hiring techs without route density can reduce profit instead of increasing it.
- A business can be “busy” and still be financially weak.
- Revenue growth is not the same as profit growth.
- Low prices can create customers, but bad customers at bad prices weaken the business.
- The best customers are usually recurring, nearby, easy to service, and easy to bill.
- The worst customers are often distant, irregular, underpriced, slow-paying, and operationally annoying.
- The owner should not expand territory just because someone is willing to pay.
- A route-based business should optimize for profit per route hour, not just total customer count.

SCORING SYSTEM
Provide a Business Health Score from 1 to 100.

Scoring meaning:

90-100:
Excellent poop scooping business fundamentals. Strong route density, strong recurring revenue, strong margins, clean billing, low owner dependency, clear service area, and scalable operations.

80-89:
Strong business with fixable weaknesses. Likely profitable and scalable if route discipline, pricing, and systems stay tight.

70-79:
Good but uneven. The business has real potential, but route density, pricing, labor leverage, billing, or marketing weaknesses are limiting profit.

60-69:
Viable but fragile. The business can work, but the owner is carrying too much operational burden, pricing risk, route inefficiency, or cash-flow stress.

50-59:
Weak structure. Revenue may exist, but the route economics, pricing, customer mix, or operations are not strong enough yet.

40-49:
High-risk business. The model likely requires major correction to become durable.

30-39:
Structurally poor. The business may survive short-term, but pricing, routes, labor, or demand are badly flawed.

1-29:
Not currently viable. Major redesign is required.

SCORING WEIGHTS
Score the business using these weighted categories:

1. Recurring Revenue Quality - 15 points
Evaluate number of recurring customers, weekly vs biweekly/monthly mix, revenue predictability, subscription behavior, and customer concentration.

2. Route Density and Territory Efficiency - 15 points
Evaluate how close customers are, travel time, service area discipline, route clustering, windshield time, and route expansion logic.

3. Gross Margin / Unit Economics - 15 points
Evaluate pricing, labor cost, fuel cost, disposal cost, payment processing, supplies, service time, and contribution margin per stop.

4. Pricing Power and Plan Structure - 10 points
Evaluate weekly, biweekly, monthly, one-time cleanup pricing, dog-count pricing, yard-size adjustments, surcharge logic, and whether prices support profit.

5. Operating Efficiency - 10 points
Evaluate scheduling, route management, field notes, tech workflow, customer updates, job completion proof, admin burden, and automation.

6. Billing and Cash Flow - 10 points
Evaluate autopay, invoicing cadence, collections, failed payments, late payments, AR aging, and owner time spent chasing money.

7. Sales and Marketing Engine - 10 points
Evaluate lead sources, local SEO, Google Business Profile, referrals, door hangers, neighborhood density marketing, follow-up, close rate, and CAC.

8. Customer Retention and Service Quality - 5 points
Evaluate churn, complaints, missed services, satisfaction, reviews, communication, and repeat behavior.

9. Scalability and Owner Dependency - 5 points
Evaluate whether the business can add techs/routes without the owner becoming the bottleneck or margins collapsing.

10. Risk Profile - 5 points
Evaluate weather risk, seasonality, staffing, service-area sprawl, customer concentration, compliance, animal/property risks, and cash-flow fragility.

Total: 100 points.
